TaxFront helps you organize documents, review AI-generated findings, and find the federal forms to prepare. Start with [Profile](https://taxfront-1e142.web.app/profile), upload documents in [Dashboard](https://taxfront-1e142.web.app/dashboard), and review the [Tax Forms Guide](https://taxfront-1e142.web.app/forms).

## Filing workflow

1. **Set your filing and residency details.** Save your filing status, tax year, and confirmed residency in Profile. The Substantial Presence Test calculator uses your countable days after applicable exclusions. The test requires at least 31 days in the tax year and 183 weighted days across three years, using weights of 1, ⅓, and ⅙. The green card test, treaty rules, and exceptions can affect your classification. [IRS Substantial Presence Test](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/substantial-presence-test)
2. **Upload your documents and check processing.** TaxFront uses Gemini to extract visible fields from W-2, 1099-NEC, 1099-INT, 1099-DIV, 1098, 1040, and Schedule C. Schedule SE and Form 8843 uploads are identified for checklist matching. Review processing status and notifications in Jobs, and verify extracted amounts against the originals.
3. **Run Analyze My Taxes.** The dashboard sends one request for the accountant and auditor reports. The accountant uses your profile filing status when available. The auditor adds the 2024 cross-form checks below. Uploads from other tax years are excluded from this checklist.
4. **Review official forms.** The Tax Forms Guide uses your confirmed status and saved year to direct you to Form 1040 for citizens and resident aliens or Form 1040-NR for nonresident aliens. If you indicated that you excluded days as an exempt individual, it also links to Form 8843. Choose the IRS revision for your filing year. TaxFront currently links to IRS forms. Filled PDF generation and return submission are outside the current workflow. [IRS prior-year forms](https://www.irs.gov/prior-year-forms-and-instructions)

## Auditor checklist

| Check | What triggers a review |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule C | Sole-proprietor income indicated by a 1099-NEC or the matching-year tax profile, with no Schedule C in the uploads for that year. |
| Schedule SE | Positive 1099-NEC compensation with no Schedule SE in the uploads. Review the $400 net-earnings threshold and applicable exceptions. Gross compensation alone does not establish a filing requirement. [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/interest-dividends-other-types-of-income/1099-misc-independent-contractors-and-self-employed/1099-misc-independent-contractors-and-self-employed-3) |
| Form 8843 | The matching-year profile indicates excluded exempt-individual days, and the uploads lack Form 8843. [IRS Form 8843](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8843) |
| 1099-INT | The auditor flags positive interest shown on an uploaded 1099-INT for reconciliation. The usual $10 threshold governs payer reporting. Taxable interest can still be reportable below that amount. [IRS Topic 403](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc403) |
| W-2 | The matching-year profile or an uploaded document indicates wages and the uploads lack a W-2 for that year. |

Checklist findings cite the upload name or the tax profile. An upload gap is a prompt to review your records.
